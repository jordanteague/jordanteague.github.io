Background

I've practiced law since 2012 and co-founded Campbell Teague in 2016.

Bio

I'm a founding partner and the chief strategy officer of Campbell Teague, where I focus on the firm's long-term direction: growth, innovation, and attorney development. Inside the firm I'm the self-described "nerd in the basement," the one building the systems.

I came to law from advertising, software development, and entrepreneurship, and have practiced across transactional work and litigation, including complex business disputes and class actions. I've won a federal bench trial and its appeal and tried an international arbitration in Paris.

Today I advise emerging-technology organizations on questions without settled answers: regulatory frameworks, legal personhood, governance, and which jurisdiction's law applies. That has included advising a token issuer under the EU's MiCA regulation in its early days and serving as outside general counsel to The Graph Foundation.

The thread through all of it is making expert judgment repeatable. The firm runs on a model in which project managers lead lawyer teams, and the tools I build apply the same idea to AI agents.

J.D., Vanderbilt University Law School, where I was Senior Technology Editor of the Vanderbilt Journal of Entertainment and Technology Law. B.S., magna cum laude, in Mathematics-Economics from Furman University.
